- **Reference.** `.omx/artifacts/music-anticipation-original-20261020/assets/reference-audio.m4a`, sha256 `6987131efbeb44cc19e106b9db1d4f0b10aba55263c8f37531b973b0ef144b61` (629,846 bytes, 39.466 s, 44.1 kHz stereo AAC).

- **Passage.** Source [15.810, 25.810) s (44.1 kHz samples 697,221–1,138,221), contiguous, at the original speed and pitch.
- **Processing.** Constant gain −2.25 dB (passage true peak +1.2 → −1.1 dBTP), an 8 ms raised-cosine fade-in, a 0.20 s raised-cosine fade-out ending at 10.000 s, and a 44.1 → 48 kHz resample (ffmpeg). There is no compression, limiting, EQ, ducking, time-stretch or pitch-shift. No music is composed or synthesized, there is no voice, and there are no added sound effects.
- **Proof.**
  - `out/audio/bgm-report.json`: decoded back, the WAV matches the source passage at lag 0 with correlation 1.0 and a level ratio of exactly −2.25 dB.
  - `tools/verify.mjs`: the muxed AAC matches the WAV at lag 0 (r ≈ 0.99995).
  - On the M4, the WAV sha256 was `cd2b6b74…f7aa`. The server's was `a8d8aad8…2546`, because platform decoding and resampling differ in the last bits. The measurements were identical.
- **Measured cues in `src/cues.js`, used by the picture:**

## Science
The captions pose the question and describe the staged experience. They contain no claim of a measured effect.

Background for the full film, with its limits:
- Salimpoor 2011, doi:10.1038/nn.2726: anticipation and pleasure. Small PET sample of self-chosen music; no dopamine numbers.
- Cheung 2019, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2019.09.067: an uncertainty × surprise interaction in chord sequences, not this song.
- Madison & Schiölde 2017: repetition can increase liking. No "always", no addiction.
- Senn 2024: no universal inverted-U law for groove.
