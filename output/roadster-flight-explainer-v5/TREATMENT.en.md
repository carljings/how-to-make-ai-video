# Roadster v5 — 「空气现形」 AIR, REVEALED — treatment

Director: Opus 5.5 (creative ownership delegated by the human, 2026-10-18). Codex: tools, local render, independent QA.
Film language: Chinese, Douyin, 9:16 native 1080×1920, 30 fps. Target length ≈ 52 s (set by the measured voice).

## 1. Why the rejected v4 frames lose the thumb

I looked at the actual encoded frames (`references/rejected-v4/`). Technically correct, emotionally inert:

1. **A poster, not a moment.** Frame 0 (sample-01) is a still product render under a giant headline. The car sits on a grey board in a black void, so it reads as a toy. There is no height, no floor, no danger, and the title is the biggest object in the frame.
2. **No world.** Every shot is the car or slab in an empty black void. At phone size about 70 % of each frame is flat black; the rest is mid-grey. There is no atmosphere, light, reflection, depth or scale.
3. **The physics is drawn, not shown.** Thin red, white and gold vectors and labels sit on top of static side views (sample-07, -18, -24). The viewer has to read a textbook diagram. Nothing in the picture *does* the pushing.
4. **Dead middle.** 5.5–17.8 s are near-static cross-sections, which is exactly where a viewer swipes. Cuts land on stills rather than on beats.
5. **Weak contrast, too many codes.** Gold, ice, red-orange, white and crimson all fight in one small frame, at low saturation.
6. **It ends on a diagram** (sample-24 chronophotograph) instead of a feeling.

## 2. The idea

**The air is the hidden protagonist.** Everyone sees a car stuck to a ceiling. We show the thing nobody can see: the air holding it there. Then the ceiling ends, and the car has to spend air of its own.

The film runs in **two realities seen through one camera**:

- **夜 NIGHT — what the eye sees.** Premium car-commercial cinematography.
  - A black arena with a colossal concrete ramp and crimson neon edges.
  - Floodlight god-rays in haze, a wet-black reflective floor, anamorphic flares.
  - A pearl-white wedge car with a long black canopy, LED bars lit.
  - **Falling dust is gravity made visible:** it always drifts straight down. No white gravity arrows are needed until the one moment we compare forces.
- **气 AIR VISION — what the air does.** Triggered by **the Ping**, a sonar-like spherical wavefront that expands from the car.
  - Behind the wavefront, solids become dark smoked glass with thin light edges.
  - The air becomes a dense, luminous, fizzing medium.
  - Two colours carry the whole argument, complementary and readable at phone size:
    - **GOLD = borrowed air pressure** pushing from outside;
    - **ICE CYAN = the car's own carried gas.**

Pressure is shown as what it is: countless tiny impacts (gold glints) on the outside of the body. Where the fans empty the gap, the glints stop. Then the many small pushes **converge into one gold force beam** (object identity: many becomes one). That same beam keeps its identity through the whole ramp: it rotates with the surface while the dust keeps falling straight down.

## 3. First seconds — promise and payoff

- **0.0 s (frame 0 already has the money shot).** A wide lens just below the deck. The car hangs upside-down on the ceiling, filling the upper frame. Fifteen metres below are the floor and floodlight pools. Dust glitters as it falls through the beams. Sound is already hitting: sub thump and fan whine.
- **0.25–3.0 s.** A slow vertigo dolly-zoom deepens the drop. The voice says 「连灰都往下掉，它凭什么不掉？」. On screen the characters 「连灰都往下掉」 literally fall away like the dust, while 「它凭什么不掉？」 stays pinned.
  - **The promise:** an impossible image, plus a question you can feel.
- **3–6 s, the wrong answer.** A close, low dolly along the flank (tyres pressed into the ceiling, the thin slit between floor and ceiling) as the fans spin up: 「你可能以为，它是被吸住的。」 The glyph 吸 cracks.
- **6–9 s, the twist and the first payoff.**
  - 「其实，是空气把它压上去的。」
  - On 空气: **the Ping.** The world flips to air vision.
  - On 压: a gold storm of impacts hits the outer body, a single beam slams up into the ceiling, and the bass hits.

Every later section opens the next loop:

| Section | Question | Payoff |
|---|---|---|
| How exactly? (9–19 s) | — | The fans pull the slit empty; outside presses hard, inside barely; the car is pinned; the loaded tyres grip. |
| Everywhere on the ramp? (19–24 s) | — | A fast air-vision replay of the climb: the gold beam turns from horizontal on the wall to straight up on the ceiling, while the dust still falls down. |
| And when the ramp ends? (24–37 s) | The free end of the deck. | The slit opens, gold dies, and the car drops. Then an X-ray of cyan stored gas, jets down, reaction up; it rolls upright into its cloud. |
| Bonus (37–45 s) | **A separate demonstration.** | A hard black separator. The car flips over a lineup; frozen air vision shows off-centre thrust as a twist around the centre of mass. Thin lines stay visible: 「画面中可见细线，作用未公开」. |
| Close (45–52 s) | — | Gold on the ramp is borrowed; cyan in the air is spent. The cyan reserve visibly drains: 「自己的气，总有喷完的时候。」 Last image: the car small in its settling cloud under the towering ramp. |

## 4. Cinematic grammar

- **The camera never parks.**
  - Crane moves, macro dollies along the slit, and a bullet-time orbit with frozen dust.
  - Speed ramps: real speed, then slow motion on the curl and the lip, then snap back.
  - Cuts land on score beats.
- **Word-driven moments** (a Hypit lesson). Visual events are keyed to aligned word times from the real voice, not fixed seconds:
  - the Ping on 空气;
  - the slam on 压;
  - the gap opening on 敞开;
  - the jets on 喷.

  If the voice changes, the events move with it.
- **Typography is sparse and kinetic.** There are only three big type moments (往下掉 falls, 吸 cracks into 压, and the closing 借 / 花 pair). Everything else is clean captions in the Douyin-safe band. Small honest slates are always present:
  - CG重建 · 据特斯拉 2026.10.16 发布的视频
  - 原理示意 · 非实拍
- **Sound:**
  - an original procedural score, a dark electronic pulse with trailer-style hits and risers;
  - silence used as a weapon before the Ping and the drop;
  - air-vision shimmer, fan whine, cold-gas hiss and impact layers;
  - final mix at −14 LUFS, ≤ −1.5 dBTP.

## 5. Facts kept (non-negotiable)

- **Ramp:** the corrected v4 fit (wall, curl, finite level deck back over the approach, free end), 6 mm floor separation, and v4 source-fitted climb and airborne poses. There is no new photogrammetry.
- **Car:** the existing inferred silver-white wedge with a long black canopy (approximate, not factory CAD).
- **Physics:**
  - Gravity is always world-down.
  - The fans lower the pressure in the nearby slit, so the outside air pushes the car toward the surface. That normal load is what lets the tyres grip.
  - The push is not propulsion, not suction, not antigravity and not maglev.
  - Off the surface, stored gas gives an opposite reaction; thrust off the centre of mass gives a torque.
  - There are no flames and no sustained-flight claim.
- **Kickflip:** a separate demonstration, with the cables visible and their load role unknown.
- **Labels:** fans, vessels, nozzles, particles, glints and beams are schematic and labelled as such. There are no performance numbers and no dimensions on screen. Sources were published 2026-10-16, so the film never says "today".

## 6. How it is made

The look needs GPU particles, sonar wavefronts and shader-level "two realities" mixing, which are the browser's home turf. So v5 uses **one custom deterministic WebGL2 engine** (`web/`), with no runtime dependencies:

- Every frame is a pure function of the frame number:
  - camera, car pose, particles (analytic, hashed; no simulation state), glints, beams, text;
  - temporal sub-frame accumulation for motion blur and anti-aliasing;
  - HDR bloom, anamorphic streak, grain, CA and grade.
- **Inputs reused, not refitted:** the car mesh and ramp/pose functions are exported from the v4 pure-Python geometry (`geometry/export_v5.py`) to JSON/binary.
- **Rendering:** a zero-dependency Node script drives headless Chrome via CDP, frame by frame, to PNGs. ffmpeg encodes. On Codex's Mac: Chrome with ANGLE/Metal. On this server: SwiftShader, used only for my own spot checks.
- **Audio:** a Node DSP script writes the score and SFX from the same timeline module the picture uses.
- **Voice:** new lines with IDs in `narration/lines.zh-CN.json`; Codex synthesizes and aligns them. The fonts are the bundled Noto Sans SC subset, re-subset by Codex for the new characters.

Blender is not needed for v5 unless the WebGL car fails a pixel review.
