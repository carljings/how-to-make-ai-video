**Its craft.** One visual grammar is used throughout:
- dark ground;
- white text with one orange accent word;
- one bold two-line headline per idea, at the top;
- the song's timeline as a recurring spine at the bottom;
- one focal diagram that transforms step by step;
- annotations that appear at the moment the claim is made;
- small "示意" and source footers.

It is 2D and simple, and it works because **every moving thing is a claim**. It runs experience → explanation → experience.

**What not to copy.**
- "你的大脑比耳朵先爽" as literal physiology.
- "drop 还没来，多巴胺先来了" as if it were measured in the viewer.
- 2.11 vs 2.51 presented as pleasure or dopamine. They are subjective ratings from a small self-chosen-music study.
- "高潮前 15 秒" as a biological timer. It was an analysis window.
- A "你的大脑" meter that looks live.

## Why our two previews failed

- **v1 (等它落下来) and the remake plan** explained, but leaned on narration and dense subtitles. The visual itself (a sphere or brain sketch) did not carry the mechanism.
- **8 s Clawd and 10 s v2 (dominoes)** were pretty analogies with no bridge. The text only asked a question and described waiting; nothing showed **what the brain does with the music** or why that matters. Spectacle stood in for explanation, and the character and world took the attention the claim needed.

## The lesson for v3

The picture itself must show the mechanism, using the song's real timeline:
- **What has actually sounded:** measured onsets and loudness.
- **What is expected next:** a conceptual prediction marker, extrapolated from the measured rhythm.
- **When the two meet, miss, or meet hugely at the drop.**

Clawd is the small viewer-proxy who reacts. Headlines are short and appear when the claim does. The payoff is the drop landing exactly on the predicted beat.

## Science boundaries for the preview

- **Claim.** When sounds repeat regularly, the brain picks up the regularity and forms an expectation of when the next sound comes. The 10 s preview teaches this one claim.
  - Fujioka et al. 2012, *J Neurosci*, doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4107-11.2012: MEG, 12 listeners; beta-band timing consistent with predicting the next sound.
  - Wacongne et al. 2011, *PNAS*, doi:10.1073/pnas.1117807108: auditory regularity and omission responses support predictions of upcoming sounds.
- **The missing eighth** is described as measured: no new onset where the pattern would put one (spacing 0.53 s instead of 0.267 s). The music is not silent there.
- **The prediction graphics are 示意.** They are not simulated MEG or EEG, there is no dopamine meter, and nothing says this track produces a fixed response in every viewer.
- **Expectation is one contributor to 上头**, not the whole explanation. The ending says so ("原因之一").

## Full-film outline (≈40 s, not implemented)

1. **0–10 s: the rule and the payoff** (this preview). Regular onsets lead to "大脑开始猜下一拍". A missing onset shows you were waiting for it, the build makes the big beat predictable, and the drop lands on the predicted beat.
2. **10–18 s: why expectation can feel good.** Salimpoor 2011, a small study with self-chosen chill music: PET dopamine release in the striatum, with fMRI separating anticipation (caudate) from the peak (nucleus accumbens). Shown as a schematic with two phases on one time axis, labelled 示意. No universal timer, no "every listener".
3. **18–26 s: not too predictable, not random.** Cheung 2019, chord sequences: pleasure depends on uncertainty and surprise *together*. Low uncertainty with a surprise and high uncertainty with a confirmation both rated as more pleasant. It is not a rule that "surprise is always better", and there is no 70/30 formula.
4. **26–32 s: familiarity.** Madison & Schiölde 2017: repeated exposure can increase liking. This tune's repeated bars are shown from measurements. No addiction claim.
5. **32–40 s: back to the song.** The last drop with a one-bar extra wait, which the viewer now predicts. The ending answers 为什么上头 with a combination: learnable rhythm, delayed and confirmed expectation, and familiarity. It is "一部分原因", and the user's playful name 开智 is kept without any IQ claim.
